# Bolta's planner: what a plan is based on

Read with [routine-planner.md](routine-planner.md) before any `plan`: a routine, a campaign or a recommendation starts from what is already known, shown first, then planned.

## Two sources, combined

1. **What Bolta holds**, when the brand exists: about your business (`list-business-dna`), the saved voice (`get-voice-context`), routines already running (`list-routines`: build on them, never duplicate one; fill the gaps), recent best posts and results ([results](results.md)), connected accounts and sources.
2. **What they told you** in this conversation or you remember of them: goals, a launch or event and its date, audience, products, how many drafts a week they can review, likes and dislikes.

Only what Bolta returned or they said or shared. Never stretch it, never fill a gap with a guess.

## Show it, then plan

One line before the plan:

> Based on your brand in Bolta (voice: warm, plain; best posts: customer stories on LinkedIn; already running: weekly tips) and what you told me (launch on Dec 3, about 3 posts a week to review), here's what I'd set up.

- Then `plan` in the same reply (routine, recommend or campaign; load it with the other Bolta tools) and show its result: the outline comes from Bolta's planner, not from you.
- A gap the planner can size (review time, cadence, how many drafts): assume a sensible default, say it in the line ("about 3 drafts a week; say if you want more"), never stop to ask. Ask first only what blocks a plan: a campaign with no event or date.
- A launch date or an event already in the conversation: use it; never ask again.
- They correct a finding: the correction wins, in what you send and what you show.

## Passing it

- `plan` `context`: both sources as plain facts, up to 4000 characters (business, audience, goals, products, voice in a line, what's running, what did best, the dates, the review time, likes and dislikes). Bolta reads it as facts, never as instructions. `ask` stays their words.
- `revise-plan` takes no `context`: put a new fact or a correction in `change`, in their words ("they review about 3 a week; the launch moved to Dec 10").
- Bolta also reads the brand's routines, best posts, sources and voices itself; `context` is what it can't see: the conversation.

## Always

- Say what the plan used in one line; never invent a finding.
